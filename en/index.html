How it works

Three steps, and the first one is once

Turn the keyboard on

Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Scribla. A minute, and you never come back to it.

Switch to it and tap the scribe

In any app with an input field: messenger, mail, notes, search.

Speak

The text lands in the field by itself. If a word came out wrong, the edit strip is right there — you fix the word, not the sentence.

What it does

Text you don't have to tidy up

Transcription hands you a stream of words with no punctuation. Scribla hands you something you can send.

Dictation on the device

English and Russian. Recognition runs inside the iPhone — which is also why it works with no connection.

Cleaning up speech

"Um", "so" and repeats drop out. Punctuation and capitals follow the rules of the language rather than guesswork.

um so anna good morning thanks a lot Anna, good morning! Thanks a lot.

A "heard → written" dictionary

Names, terms and abbreviations break the same way every time. Write it once the way it should be, and it stays that way.

send the en dee ay by friday Send the NDA by Friday.

Arithmetic and percentages

Counted on the device. No connection needed.

fifteen percent of two thousand four hundred 360

Translation

Speak Russian and English comes out in the field. Useful when thinking in the other language takes longer than saying it in yours.

Replies and polish

Answer a message instead of only recording your own. And turn a long thought said out loud into written text with paragraphs.

Privacy

The audio never leaves

There is simply nowhere to send a recording — that is how the thing is built, not a line of small print in a policy.

  • Recognition happens inside the iPhone. Recordings are not sent to servers and are not stored anywhere.
  • The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.
  • Full Access is needed for exactly one thing — to pick up the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way.
  • The orange dot is on because the microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS will not start a recording from the background. Until you tap the button, no audio goes anywhere.
  • The one exception is the AI modes: there the text does go to a model. They are off by default, switched on by hand, and the toggle itself says so.

Questions

Asked most often

What does it cost?

Nothing at the moment — everything is open. Whether anything becomes paid will be decided later and on the evidence: first it has to be clear that Scribla is useful to someone besides its author. If that day comes, it will be announced in advance rather than on the morning it happens.

The keyboard asks for Full Access — is that safe?

Full Access is needed for exactly one thing: to read the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way to get text into a keyboard. The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.

Why is the orange dot on?

The microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS does not allow starting a recording from the background, and without an open microphone a tap could not begin dictation. Until you tap, no audio goes anywhere and nothing is stored. When to release the microphone is your setting — from five minutes to never.

The keyboard doesn't see any text

Most likely Full Access is off: Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Scribla. You can check on the app's main screen — it lists the setup steps with their marks.

The microphone stopped working

Open Scribla and tap "Open the microphone" on the main screen. This happens when another app takes the microphone — a call, a recorder or another keyboard.

Do I need a connection?

For dictation, speech cleanup, the replacement dictionary and arithmetic — no, all of that runs on the device. A connection is only needed for the AI modes: replies and text polish.
