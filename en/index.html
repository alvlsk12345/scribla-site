In any app with an input field: messenger, mail, notes, search.

Speak

Text you don’t have to tidy up

Voice input usually hands you a stream of words with no punctuation. Scribla hands you something you can send.

Dictation on the device

English, Russian, Spanish and Chinese. Recognition runs inside the device itself — the phone or the Mac — which is also why it works with no connection.

Cleaning up speech

“Um”, “so” and repeats drop out. Punctuation and capitals follow the rules of the language rather than guesswork.

um so anna good morning thanks a lot Anna, good morning! Thanks a lot.

A “heard → written” dictionary

Names, terms and abbreviations break the same way every time. Write it once the way it should be, and it stays that way.

Replies and polish

Answer a message instead of only recording your own. And turn a long thought said out loud into written text with paragraphs.

On the Mac it’s the same thing, on a key

A separate app: an icon in the menu bar and a window with three panes. Hold the right ⌘, speak, and the text lands wherever the cursor is, in any window. Nothing to switch to.

  • hold the right ⌘ what you said
  • hold the right ⌘ ⌥ a reply to what you said
  • the same, then press / a translation of it

Modifier keys rather than a letter combination: you can dictate mid-sentence, one-handed and without looking. The right ⌘ stays an ordinary modifier — if you were reaching for a shortcut, the recording is dropped without a trace and ⌘C goes through untouched. The mode can change mid-phrase: start talking, add ⌥, and the same words go out as a question.

Dictation and the dictionary work offline. Replies and translation are computed by a model elsewhere — the same AI modes as on the phone, and they switch off in the same place.

The audio never leaves

There is simply nowhere to send a recording — that is how the app is built, not a line of small print in a policy.

  • Recognition happens inside the device itself — the phone or the Mac. Recordings are not sent to servers and are not stored anywhere.
  • The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.
  • Full Access is needed for exactly one thing — to pick up the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way.
  • The orange dot is on because the microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS will not start a recording from the background. Until you tap the button, no audio goes anywhere.
  • One caveat, and only one: audio never leaves, but text does in the AI modes. Those modes are on from the first launch. Replies and polish are computed by a model elsewhere, and they do not work offline. The intro says what goes out, directly above the switch. You can turn them off there, or later in the settings.

    Asked most often

    What does it cost?

    Nothing at the moment — everything is open. Whether anything becomes paid will be decided later and on the evidence: first it has to be clear that Scribla is useful to someone besides us. If that day comes, we will say so in advance rather than on the morning it happens.

    The keyboard asks for Full Access — is that safe?

    Full Access is needed for exactly one thing: to read the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way to get text into a keyboard. The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.

    How does the Mac version differ from the phone one?

    In how you call it. On the phone Scribla is a keyboard: you switch to it and tap the scribe. On the Mac it is an app in the menu bar that hears the right ⌘ in any window, so there is nothing to switch to. Recognition, speech cleanup, the dictionary and the AI modes are the same. There are no emoji or word-by-word edit panels on the Mac: you have a real keyboard there.

    Why does Scribla on the Mac want Accessibility?

    For exactly two things: to hear that you are holding the key, and to insert the finished text where the cursor is. macOS offers no other way. The app does not read your screen and goes nowhere with that permission. If the switch is on and Scribla still does not hear the key, remove the row with “−” in the list and launch the app again: the system’s record was made under an earlier signature, and toggling it off and on does not fix that.

    Why is the orange dot on?

    The microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS does not allow starting a recording from the background, and without an open microphone a tap could not begin dictation. Until you tap, no audio goes anywhere and nothing is stored. When to release the microphone is your setting — from five minutes to never.

    Do I need a connection?

    For dictation, speech cleanup, the replacement dictionary and arithmetic — no, all of that runs on the device. A connection is only needed for the AI modes: replies and text polish.

    Can I support the work?

    Yes — just above, with any card. For a long time there was no honest way to say that: the only channel ran through the Russian instant-payment system, which works from Russian banks and nowhere else. There is a second one now. Writing in above and telling us what is broken is still worth more, and it costs nothing.
