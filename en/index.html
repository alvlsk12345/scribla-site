Where it earns its keep, every day

Not “another dictation app”, but a way not to type where typing is awkward, slow, or in the wrong language. And all of it in the field you are already in.

  • on the move, one hand say the phrase, and the message goes out to the chat with punctuation and capitals, no “um” and “so”
  • a thought longer than the screen talk the way you think, five minutes if need be: the polish mode turns it into text with paragraphs, not a wall
  • reply to an email the “text from the field” key attaches the email to your question: “say Thursday won’t work, offer Friday” — and the finished reply is in the field
  • do the sums in a chat “fifteen percent of two thousand four hundred” — 360 in the field; no calculator to open
  • write in a language you don’t speak say it in English, and it lands in Spanish, Russian or Chinese
  • ask without leaving the chat a rate, a date, what was decided about the flight — the model looks it up on the web and answers right in the field, with the sources kept in history
  • names and terms recognition keeps writing “en dee ay”? Fix it once — it is NDA from then on

In any app with a text field: messenger, mail, notes, search, someone else’s form. Dictation, speech clean-up, the dictionary and arithmetic run on the device itself, offline; answers and polish are done by the model — and you don’t pay for it.

A keyboard, not a separate window

Scribla doesn’t ask you to come over, dictate, and carry the result back. It takes the place of your ordinary keyboard: in a messenger, an email, a note, a search box, someone else’s form. Tap the scribe, speak, tap again, and the text is already in the field.

Four modes, and the mode decides what lands in the field

  • Text what you said, with punctuation and capitals
  • Math the answer instead of the sum: say “fifteen percent of two thousand four hundred” and 360 lands
  • Translate said in one language, written in another
  • AI an answer to your question, not a record of it: the model searches the web and answers from what it finds

The modes sit in a row of their own and they are large: you aim at them with your eyes, not blindly. The one you pick holds until you change it, so there is nothing to say out loud — no “calculate”, no “translate”. Next to them is the language key: it shows which language we are listening in, and a tap moves to the next one downloaded.

Cleaning up speech

Hesitations, stumbles and repeats drop out, and punctuation and capitals follow the rules of the language rather than guesswork. Real words are not cut blindly: “actually”, “right”, “so” stay where they carry meaning.

Answers from a model

Answer a message instead of only recording your own: ask out loud and the reply lands in the field, built from pages found on the web just then. The same model turns a long thought said out loud into written text with paragraphs. What the model does is set out below.

You asked — the answer is in the field, not the question

AI mode is not a separate chat you leave for and come back from with something in the clipboard. Same scribe, same keyboard: say the question out loud and the answer lands right in the email, message or note you were already in.

  • a question out loud before answering we search the web and answer from the pages we found — so a rate, a date or yesterday’s news come out current instead of coming out of the model’s memory. Where the answer came from is visible in the app’s history: a line saying “Answered from”, with links
  • a follow-up “shorter”, “in Russian”, “no, I meant Kazan” — the answer is rewritten in place, no need to ask again. Reshaping the wording needs no connection; if the follow-up asks for new facts (“and in euros?”), we search again
  • the text in the field its own key attaches whatever is already written in the field to your question: reply to an email, make sense of someone’s message, carry on a paragraph
  • polish a long thought said out loud becomes written text: paragraphs, agreement, no stumbles. The model matches the markup to wherever the text is going — asterisks and hashes for Obsidian, plain text for an ordinary field
  • emoji on a long press the model places them by the sense of the whole phrase, not by the last word

On the Mac these are the same modes and the same answers, called by keys instead: ⌘⌥ on the right for an answer, the same two plus / for a translation. Nothing to learn twice.

What leaves the device

  • Audio never
  • The question text in the AI modes
  • The route through scribla.io
  • Your own key OpenAI, DeepSeek

The built-in service’s key lives on our server, not inside the app: from inside the app anyone who wants it can take it. We store neither the question nor the answer — the service record gets a length and a response code. With your own key the text goes to the service you picked directly, past us.

The AI modes are on from the first launch, and the walkthrough says so right above the switch. You can turn them off there, or later in the settings.

No subscription, no account, no card: the AI modes are included, and the bill for the model comes to us, not to you.

How long it stays that way we honestly cannot say: first we need to find out whether Scribla is of any use to anyone besides us. If something ever does become paid, we will say so in advance rather than on the day it is switched on.

Everything else has nothing to do with the model at all: recognition, speech cleanup, the replacement dictionary and arithmetic are done by the device itself and need no connection. The model is called only where nothing else will do — which is why Scribla keeps writing on a plane.

The audio never leaves

There is simply nowhere to send a recording — that is how the app is built, not a line of small print in a policy.

  • Recognition happens inside the device itself — the phone or the Mac. Recordings are not sent to servers and are not stored anywhere.
  • The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.
  • Full Access is needed for exactly one thing — to pick up the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way.
  • The orange dot is on because the microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS will not start a recording from the background. Until you tap the button, no audio goes anywhere.
  • One caveat, and only one: audio never leaves, but text does in the AI modes. Those modes are on from the first launch. Replies and polish are computed by a model elsewhere, and they do not work offline. The intro says what goes out, directly above the switch. You can turn them off there, or later in the settings.

What else is on the keyboard

There aren’t many keys, and each one covers the case that would otherwise send you off to another keyboard and out of sight of the field.

  • the scribe, the record key tap, speak, tap again. The ring around him says whether we are listening or already working it out, and the badge says which mode is on
  • the edit strip it comes up above the keys by itself: first the sentences you spoke, tap a sentence and it opens into words, tap a word and you get its cases, number and capitalisation. One wrong form is no longer a reason to retype the phrase
  • “remember the spelling” the word goes from the edit strip into your own dictionary and is written your way from then on
  • the row of marks and digits without leaving for another layout. The row is deliberately shorter than the others: commas and periods the model places itself, while question and exclamation marks depend on intonation and come out worse, so those you add by hand
  • the space bar hold it and slide: sideways the cursor walks letter by letter, up and down it moves whole words. You can’t land a finger between two letters; this way you can
  • the two arrows the left one removes the whole last insertion, the right one brings it back. Hold them and it goes one sentence at a time: the one the cursor sits in leaves whole, with no holding down backspace
  • emoji our own panel: a keyboard extension cannot summon the system one. Hold the key and the model places emoji by meaning across the whole phrase you just spoke
  • ABC and АБВ ordinary letters, to finish a word by hand without leaving the scribe
  • the arrow leaving a square your apps as tiles with their real icons: tap one and you are there, and the field stays open. The set is assembled in settings beforehand, because iOS tells no one which apps are open right now
  • the gear Scribla settings

There are places where no third-party keyboard appears at all: banks and government apps block them entirely, and no one can get around that. For those, dictate on Scribla’s own main screen — the text lands in the clipboard and the buttons below take you to the app, where a long press pastes it.

The app listens, the keyboard writes

Recognition is done by the app, not the keyboard: iOS doesn’t allow an extension that much. So don’t swipe Scribla away in the app switcher — swipe it away and the scribe falls asleep, and your first tap goes on waking him. Everything else lives in the app’s settings.

  • Languages. A pair works at a time: the main one and the additional one. The language key moves between them, translation runs between them, and the “you seem to have switched language” hint compares exactly this pair.
  • Your own dictionary. On the left, what gets heard; on the right, what should be written. Case on the left doesn’t matter.
  • Polish. It breaks a wall of dictation into paragraphs, fixes agreement, removes stumbles. A model elsewhere computes it, as it does the answers in AI mode: without a connection neither works, and both switch off in one place.
  • Markup delivery. Where the text is going: asterisks and hashes for Obsidian, Notion and Bear; an underline instead of bold for ordinary fields, which have no typeface at all; plain text for anywhere.
  • Translation. The system downloads language packs only while the app is open — from a keyboard it won’t even show the prompt. With the pack, the phone translates by itself and offline; without it, the translation goes over the network.
  • History. The text only, and only the latest entries. Audio is never stored: it lives in memory and gets overwritten by the next take.
  • Your numbers. How many words you said and how much time that saved. It is an estimate rather than a measurement, and the whole arithmetic is shown right there, including what doesn’t count.
  • Microphone. When to release it is yours to set, from five minutes to never. Auto-stop on silence is a separate switch, with the limit for a single recording next to it.
  • Diagnostics. Service notes about the app’s own work: what you pressed, where it stumbled. No audio, none of your words, and it switches off.

There is a longer walkthrough inside the app — “How it works”: what sits where on the keyboard, and why it was done that way.

    Asked most often

    What does it cost?

    Nothing at the moment — everything is open, the AI modes included: answers, web search and polish. The key is ours and the bill for the model comes to us; no subscription, no account, no card. Whether anything becomes paid will be decided later and on the evidence: first it has to be clear that Scribla is useful to someone besides us. If that day comes, we will say so in advance rather than on the morning it happens.

    How is this better than the built-in dictation?

    The built-in one writes for you too. On top of that Scribla drops the “ums” and repeats, remembers your own dictionary of names and terms, does arithmetic, translates and answers questions — all of it in the field you are in, without leaving for another app. Four languages are recognised on the device itself, offline included, and on the Mac it hears its key in any window. And it has a scribe.

    Which model answers, and can I bring my own?

    By default the built-in service answers, and the route is this: the question goes to our server at scribla.io, from there we search the web and ask the model on our key. The key sits on the server, not inside the app — from inside the app anyone who wants it can take it. We store neither the question nor the answer. If you have your own OpenAI or DeepSeek key, put it in the settings: the text will then go to them directly, past us.

    Can I use it with no AI at all?

    Yes. The AI modes are on from the first launch, and the walkthrough says so right above the switch — you can decline there, or later in the settings. What remains is dictation, speech cleanup, the replacement dictionary, arithmetic and translation with the language pack downloaded: all of it computed by the device itself, with no connection needed.

    The keyboard asks for Full Access — is that safe?

    Full Access is needed for exactly one thing: to read the recognised text from the shared container, where the Scribla app itself put it. iOS offers no other way to get text into a keyboard. The keyboard never goes online and does not read what you type on ordinary keys.

    How does the Mac version differ from the phone one?

    In how you call it. On the phone Scribla is a keyboard: you switch to it and tap the scribe. On the Mac it is an app in the menu bar that hears the right ⌘ in any window, so there is nothing to switch to. Recognition, speech cleanup, the dictionary and the AI modes are the same. There are no emoji or word-by-word edit panels on the Mac: you have a real keyboard there.

    Why does Scribla on the Mac want Accessibility?

    For exactly two things: to hear that you are holding the key, and to insert the finished text where the cursor is. macOS offers no other way. The app does not read your screen and goes nowhere with that permission. If the switch is on and Scribla still does not hear the key, remove the row with “−” in the list and launch the app again: the system’s record was made under an earlier signature, and toggling it off and on does not fix that.

    Why is the orange dot on?

    The microphone is opened ahead of time: iOS does not allow starting a recording from the background, and without an open microphone a tap could not begin dictation. Until you tap, no audio goes anywhere and nothing is stored. When to release the microphone is your setting — from five minutes to never.

    Do I need a connection?

    Dictation, speech cleanup, the replacement dictionary and arithmetic run on the device. So does translation, once the language pack is downloaded; without it, translation goes over the network. That leaves currency rates, AI answers and text polish.

    How much space does it take?

    A recognition model weighs between 137 and 326 MB, depending on the language. None ships inside the app: it downloads for the languages you choose, and two of them do — the main one and the additional one. The rest take up nothing until you pick them.

    My bank’s app doesn’t list Scribla among the keyboards

    Banks and government apps block third-party keyboards entirely, not just ours. That is their right, and no one can get around it. Dictate on Scribla’s own main screen instead: the text lands in the clipboard by itself, and the buttons below take you to the app, where a long press pastes it.

    Can I support the work?

    Yes — just above, with any card. For a long time there was no honest way to say that: the only channel ran through the Russian instant-payment system, which works from Russian banks and nowhere else. There is a second one now. Writing in above and telling us what is broken is still worth more, and it costs nothing.
